September 2019

NeurIPS 2019 decisions: convex relaxations come under scrutiny, with an exact study revealing an inherent barrier and k-ReLU's multi-neuron relaxations moving beyond it. Randomized smoothing gains adversarial training and tighter certificates, and other directions include DEEPG for geometric robustness, certificates for graph perturbations, GeoCert's exact pointwise robustness, SDP-based Lipschitz bounds and Neural Lyapunov Control.

15 NNV papers accepted in September 2019, at NeurIPS 2019.

  • NeurIPS 2019 Bound propagation & abstract interpretation A Convex Relaxation Barrier to Tight Robustness Verification of Neural Networks (arXiv)
    Hadi Salman, Greg Yang, Huan Zhang, Cho-Jui Hsieh, Pengchuan Zhang

    Unifies LP-relaxed verifiers under one convex relaxation framework with primal-dual strong duality, then solves the optimal relaxation exactly using over 22 CPU-years, finding it barely narrows the gap to PGD on MNIST and CIFAR networks: an inherent barrier.

  • NeurIPS 2019 Bound propagation & abstract interpretation Abstraction based Output Range Analysis for Neural Networks (arXiv)
    Pavithra Prabhakar, Zahra Rahimi Afzal

    Computes output ranges of ReLU feed-forward networks by abstracting them into smaller interval neural networks with interval weights that over-approximate the output range, then solving a mixed integer linear program; experiments show the time-precision trade-off.

  • NeurIPS 2019 Bound propagation & abstract interpretation Beyond the Single Neuron Convex Barrier for Neural Network Certification
    Gagandeep Singh, Rupanshu Ganvir, Markus Püschel, Martin Vechev

    k-ReLU computes convex relaxations of multiple ReLUs in a layer jointly rather than one at a time, overcoming the single-neuron triangle relaxation barrier; combined with existing verifiers, it certifies significantly more precisely while staying scalable.

  • NeurIPS 2019 Beyond feed-forward netsBeyond ℓp input properties Certifiable Robustness to Graph Perturbations (arXiv)
    Aleksandar Bojchevski, Stephan Günnemann

    The first method to certify robustness and non-robustness of graph neural networks and label/feature propagation to graph structure perturbations, computing certificates efficiently via connections to PageRank and Markov decision processes, plus robust training that increases certifiably robust nodes.

  • NeurIPS 2019 Probabilistic certification Certified Adversarial Robustness with Additive Noise (arXiv)
    Bai Li, Changyou Chen, Wenlin Wang, Lawrence Carin

    Connects adversarial robustness to additive random noise to obtain scalable certified bounds on input perturbation norms, plus a training strategy that enlarges these bounds; on MNIST, CIFAR-10 and ImageNet it competes with provable defense methods.

  • NeurIPS 2019 Beyond ℓp input propertiesBound propagation & abstract interpretation Certifying Geometric Robustness of Neural Networks
    Mislav Balunovic, Maximilian Baader, Gagandeep Singh, Timon Gehr, Martin Vechev

    DEEPG certifies robustness to geometric transformations such as rotation and scaling by computing sound, asymptotically optimal linear relaxations for any composition of transformations through sampling plus optimization, certifying more complex transformations than prior methods on large networks.

  • NeurIPS 2019 OtherComplete verification Computing Linear Restrictions of Neural Networks (arXiv)
    Matthew Sotoudeh, Aditya V. Thakur

    ExactLine exactly computes the restriction of a piecewise-linear network to a line segment, partitioning it into affine regions; used to find exact ACAS Xu decision boundaries, compute exact integrated gradients and test a hypothesis about adversarial examples.

  • NeurIPS 2019 Bound propagation & abstract interpretation Efficient and Accurate Estimation of Lipschitz Constants for Deep Neural Networks (arXiv)
    Mahyar Fazlyab, Alexander Robey, Hamed Hassani, Manfred Morari, George J. Pappas

    Computes guaranteed upper bounds on deep network Lipschitz constants via a semidefinite program, modelling activations as gradients of convex potentials with quadratic constraints; gives the most accurate bounds in experiments on random, MNIST and Iris networks, usable for robustness guarantees.

  • NeurIPS 2019 NN control systems Neural Lyapunov Control (arXiv)
    Ya-Chien Chang, Nima Roohi, Sicun Gao

    Learns nonlinear control policies together with neural Lyapunov functions using a learner and a counterexample-finding falsifier, terminating with provable stability once no counterexample exists, and obtaining much larger regions of attraction than LQR and SOS/SDP.

  • NeurIPS 2019 Formal explanations On Relating Explanations and Adversarial Examples
    Alexey Ignatiev, Nina Narodytska, João Marques-Silva

    Shows that explanations and adversarial examples of ML models are related by a generalized hitting set duality, and gives algorithms that compute adversarial examples from explanations and vice versa.

  • NeurIPS 2019 Certified training Preventing Gradient Attenuation in Lipschitz Constrained Convolutional Networks (arXiv)
    Qiyang Li, Saminul Haque, Cem Anil, James Lucas, Roger B. Grosse, Jörn-Henrik Jacobsen

    Trains provably Lipschitz convolutional networks without gradient norm attenuation via BCOP, the Block Convolution Orthogonal Parameterization of orthogonal convolutions, competing with existing approaches for provable adversarial robustness and Wasserstein distance estimation.

  • NeurIPS 2019 Complete verification Provable Certificates for Adversarial Examples: Fitting a Ball in the Union of Polytopes (arXiv)
    Matt Jordan, Justin Lewis, Alex Dimakis

    GeoCert computes exact pointwise robustness of ReLU networks for convex ℓp norms by finding the largest ball around an input inside a polyhedral complex, returning anytime lower bounds that tighten until exact and beat prior work under time limits.

  • NeurIPS 2019 Probabilistic certificationCertified training Provably Robust Deep Learning via Adversarially Trained Smoothed Classifiers (arXiv)
    Hadi Salman, Jerry Li, Ilya P. Razenshteyn, Pengchuan Zhang, Huan Zhang, Sébastien Bubeck, Greg Yang

    Improves randomized smoothing by adversarially training smoothed classifiers with an attack adapted to them, outperforming all existing provably ℓ2-robust classifiers on ImageNet and CIFAR-10; pre-training and semi-supervised learning give further gains.

  • NeurIPS 2019 Probabilistic certification Theoretical evidence for adversarial robustness through randomization (arXiv)
    Rafael Pinot, Laurent Meunier, Alexandre Araujo, Hisashi Kashima, Florian Yger, Cédric Gouy-Pailler, Jamal Atif

    Gives theoretical grounding for noise-injection defenses: relates the randomization rate to adversarial robustness for exponential-family noise distributions and derives an upper bound on the adversarial generalization gap of randomized networks, supported by experiments.

  • NeurIPS 2019 Probabilistic certification Tight Certificates of Adversarial Robustness for Randomly Smoothed Classifiers (arXiv)
    Guang-He Lee, Yang Yuan, Shiyu Chang, Tommi S. Jaakkola

    Extends robustness certificates for randomly smoothed classifiers beyond Gaussian ℓ2 to broad distribution classes, including discrete ℓ0-bounded adversaries, and tightens guarantees using function-class assumptions such as decision trees; evaluated on image and molecule datasets.
