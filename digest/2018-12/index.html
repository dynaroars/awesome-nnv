December 2018

ICLR 2019 decisions: mixed integer programming gives exact adversarial accuracy for piecewise-linear networks, while training for ReLU stability makes verification faster. Other directions include boosting robustness certification, statistical robustness estimates via multi-level splitting, and verification of nonlinear specifications such as energy conservation in learned dynamics.

5 NNV papers accepted in December 2018, at ICLR 2019.

  • ICLR 2019 Probabilistic certification A Statistical Approach to Assessing Neural Network Robustness (arXiv)
    Stefan Webb, Tom Rainforth, Yee Whye Teh, M. Pawan Kumar

    Estimates the probability that a network property is violated under an input model with multi-level splitting, a Monte Carlo rare-event method, giving statistical robustness estimates that emulate formal verification on benchmarks while scaling to larger networks.

  • ICLR 2019 Bound propagation & abstract interpretationComplete verification Boosting Robustness Certification of Neural Networks
    Gagandeep Singh, Timon Gehr, Markus Püschel, Martin Vechev

    No summary yet: no abstract was found for this paper.

  • ICLR 2019 Complete verification Evaluating Robustness of Neural Networks with Mixed Integer Programming (arXiv)
    Vincent Tjeng, Kai Yuanqing Xiao, Russ Tedrake

    Formulates verification of piecewise-linear networks as a mixed integer program with tight formulations and a presolve step, running two to three orders of magnitude faster and finding the exact adversarial accuracy of an MNIST classifier at ℓ∞ ε=0.1.

  • ICLR 2019 Certified training Training for Faster Adversarial Robustness Verification via Inducing ReLU Stability (arXiv)
    Kai Yuanqing Xiao, Vincent Tjeng, Nur Muhammad (Mahi) Shafiullah, Aleksander Madry

    Trains networks to be both robust and easier to verify by encouraging weight sparsity and ReLU stability; sparsity makes intractable verification tractable, and ReLU stability adds a 4-13x verification speedup across training procedures and verifiers.

  • ICLR 2019 Beyond linear output propertiesBound propagation & abstract interpretation Verification of Non-Linear Specifications for Neural Networks (arXiv)
    Chongli Qin, Krishnamurthy Dvijotham, Brendan O'Donoghue, Rudy Bunel, Robert Stanforth, Sven Gowal, Jonathan Uesato, Grzegorz Swirszcz, Pushmeet Kohli

    Extends neural network verification to convex-relaxable nonlinear specifications, such as energy conservation in learned dynamics, semantic label consistency under perturbation and bounded errors when summing handwritten digits, and verifies them effectively while exposing failure modes.
