May 2025

ICML 2025 decisions: probabilistic certification leads, from a critique of average certified radius to cost-sensitive smoothing and branch-and-bound for probabilistic verification. Certified training is re-examined with CTBench and BRONet, SDP-CROWN brings SDP tightness to bound propagation, and formal explanations use abstraction-refinement; floating-point soundness of deployed verifiers is also questioned.

15 NNV papers accepted in May 2025, at ICML 2025.

ICML 2025 · Vancouver, Canada 15 / 3,240 ICML 2025 papers are NNV, by poster session

  • ICML 2025 Certified trainingOther CTBench: A Library and Benchmark for Certified Training (arXiv)
    Yuhao Mao, Stefan Balauca, Martin Vechev

    Introduces CTBench, a library and benchmark that evaluates deterministic certified training algorithms under fair schedules, certification and tuned hyperparameters; most algorithms beat their reported results, recent gains shrink against tuned baselines, and certified models show sparser activations and shared mistakes.

    Vancouver · Poster #E-2203, East Exhibition Hall A-B · Tue 15 Jul, 16:30–19:00 (Vancouver time)

  • ICML 2025 Probabilistic certificationCertified training Efficient Robust Conformal Prediction via Lipschitz-Bounded Networks (arXiv)
    Thomas Massena, Léo Andéol, Thibaut Boissin, Franck Mamalet, Corentin Friedrich, Mathieu Serrurier, Sébastien Gerchinovitz

    Estimates robust conformal prediction sets with lip-rcp using Lipschitz-bounded networks, giving smaller sets and faster computation than prior robust CP on medium and large tasks such as ImageNet, and derives worst-case coverage bounds for vanilla CP valid across all attack levels.

    Vancouver · Poster #E-2305, East Exhibition Hall A-B · Tue 15 Jul, 16:30–19:00 (Vancouver time)

  • ICML 2025 Formal explanations Explaining, Fast and Slow: Abstraction and Refinement of Provable Explanations (arXiv)
    Shahaf Bassan, Yizhak Yisrael Elboher, Tobias Ladner, Matthias Althoff, Guy Katz

    Computes provably sufficient explanations of neural network predictions faster through abstraction-refinement: a reduced network whose sufficient explanations also hold for the original speeds verification, and the network is gradually enlarged when an explanation fails, giving interpretations across abstraction levels.

    Vancouver · Poster #E-1204, East Exhibition Hall A-B · Tue 15 Jul, 16:30–19:00 (Vancouver time)

  • ICML 2025 Global & relational propertiesProbabilistic certification Probably Approximately Global Robustness Certification (arXiv)
    Peter Blohm, Patrick Indri, Thomas Gärtner, Sagar Malhotra

    Certifies a probabilistic relaxation of global robustness by sampling an ε-net and querying a local robustness oracle, with sample size independent of input dimension, class count and learning algorithm; it scales beyond formal methods and characterizes robustness better than sampling approaches.

    Vancouver · Poster #E-2200, East Exhibition Hall A-B · Tue 15 Jul, 16:30–19:00 (Vancouver time)

  • ICML 2025 Probabilistic certificationBound propagation & abstract interpretation Exact Upper and Lower Bounds for the Output Distribution of Neural Networks with Random Inputs (arXiv)
    Andrey Kofnov, Daniel Kapla, Ezio Bartocci, Efstathia Bura

    Computes guaranteed upper and lower bounds on the output CDF of neural networks with random inputs by bounding general networks with ReLU networks; bounds converge to the true CDF as resolution grows and cover CNNs and feedforward networks with activations like ReLU, tanh and softmax.

    Vancouver · Poster #W-1017, West Exhibition Hall B2-B3 · Wed 16 Jul, 11:00–13:30 (Vancouver time)

  • ICML 2025 Complete verification LEVIS: Large Exact Verifiable Input Spaces for Neural Networks (arXiv)
    Mohamad Fares El Hajj Chehade, Wenting Li, Brian Wesley Bell, Russell Bent, Saif R. Kazi, Hao Zhu

    Finds large verifiable input regions free of adversarial examples with LEVIS: LEVIS-α computes one large verifiable ball and LEVIS-β covers the space with multiple balls, using mixed-integer programming plus complementarity-constrained optimization for up to 6 times faster runtime.

    Vancouver · Poster #E-2204, East Exhibition Hall A-B · Wed 16 Jul, 11:00–13:30 (Vancouver time)

  • ICML 2025 spotlight 🎉 OtherQuantized & bit-level No Soundness in the Real World: On the Challenges of the Verification of Deployed Neural Networks (arXiv)
    Attila Szász, Balázs Bánhelyi, Márk Jelasity

    Shows that current neural network verifiers are not practically sound, since bounding full-precision outputs does not bound floating-point outputs in deployment; adversarial networks exploiting floating-point operation order and precision fool every tested verifier, and practical soundness is argued to be computationally harder.

    Vancouver · Poster #E-2200, East Exhibition Hall A-B · Wed 16 Jul, 11:00–13:30 (Vancouver time)

  • ICML 2025 spotlight 🎉 Bound propagation & abstract interpretation SDP-CROWN: Efficient Bound Propagation for Neural Network Verification with Tightness of Semidefinite Programming (arXiv)
    Hong-Ming Chiu, Hao Chen, Huan Zhang, Richard Y. Zhang

    Combines SDP tightness with bound-propagation scalability in SDP-CROWN, adding a linear bound that captures ℓ2-norm inter-neuron coupling with one extra parameter per layer, up to √n tighter than per-neuron bounds; inside α-CROWN it verifies models with 65 thousand neurons.

    Vancouver · Poster #E-2208, East Exhibition Hall A-B · Wed 16 Jul, 11:00–13:30 (Vancouver time)

  • ICML 2025 Probabilistic certification Average Certified Radius is a Poor Metric for Randomized Smoothing (arXiv)
    Chenhao Sun, Yuhao Mao, Mark Niklas Müller, Martin Vechev

    Argues average certified radius is a flawed metric for randomized smoothing, proving trivial classifiers can reach arbitrarily large ACR and that it overweights easy samples; strategies exploiting this reach top ACR on CIFAR-10, and the distribution of p_A is proposed instead.

    Vancouver · Poster #E-2206, East Exhibition Hall A-B · Wed 16 Jul, 16:30–19:00 (Vancouver time)

  • ICML 2025 Formal explanationsBeyond feed-forward nets Beyond Topological Self-Explainable GNNs: A Formal Explainability Perspective (arXiv)
    Steve Azzolin, Sagar Malhotra, Andrea Passerini, Stefano Teso

    Formalizes the minimal explanations of self-explainable GNNs and compares them to prime implicant and faithful explanations, finding they match PI explanations only for some tasks; proposes Dual-Channel GNNs combining a white-box rule extractor with an SE-GNN that recover succinct rules.

    Vancouver · Poster #E-3106, East Exhibition Hall A-B · Thu 17 Jul, 11:00–13:30 (Vancouver time)

  • ICML 2025 Certified training Certification for Differentially Private Prediction in Gradient-Based Training (arXiv)
    Matthew Wicker, Philip Sosnin, Igor Shilov, Adrianna Janik, Mark Niklas Müller, Yves-Alexandre de Montjoye, Adrian Weller, Calvin Tsay

    Improves differentially private prediction by computing dataset-specific upper bounds on prediction sensitivity with convex relaxation and bound propagation, combined with the smooth sensitivity mechanism; on medical imaging and NLP data these bounds can be orders of magnitude tighter than global sensitivity.

    Vancouver · Poster #E-1007, East Exhibition Hall A-B · Thu 17 Jul, 11:00–13:30 (Vancouver time)

  • ICML 2025 spotlight 🎉 Certified training Enhancing Certified Robustness via Block Reflector Orthogonal Layers and Logit Annealing Loss (arXiv)
    Bo-Han Lai, Pin-Han Huang, Bo-Han Kung, Shang-Tse Chen

    Improves certified robustness of Lipschitz networks with a Block Reflector Orthogonal (BRO) layer and a logit annealing loss that raises margins for most points; the resulting BRONet outperforms baselines on CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet and ImageNet.

    Vancouver · Poster #E-2500, East Exhibition Hall A-B · Thu 17 Jul, 11:00–13:30 (Vancouver time)

  • ICML 2025 Probabilistic certification Pixel-level Certified Explanations via Randomized Smoothing (arXiv)
    Alaa Anani, Tobias Lorenz, Mario Fritz, Bernt Schiele

    Certifies pixel-level robustness of any black-box attribution method against ℓ2-bounded perturbations by sparsifying and smoothing attribution maps with randomized smoothing, treated as segmentation; evaluating 12 attribution methods on 5 ImageNet models shows the certified attributions are robust, interpretable and faithful.

    Vancouver · Poster #E-2400, East Exhibition Hall A-B · Thu 17 Jul, 11:00–13:30 (Vancouver time)

  • ICML 2025 Probabilistic certificationComplete verification Solving Probabilistic Verification Problems of Neural Networks using Branch and Bound (arXiv)
    David Boetius, Stefan Leue, Tobias Sutter

    Solves probabilistic verification problems, such as demographic parity fairness, by iteratively refining lower and upper output-probability bounds with bound propagation and branch and bound; it cuts solve times from tens of minutes to tens of seconds and is sound, and complete under mild conditions.

    Vancouver · Poster #E-2302, East Exhibition Hall A-B · Thu 17 Jul, 11:00–13:30 (Vancouver time)

  • ICML 2025 Probabilistic certification Provably Cost-Sensitive Adversarial Defense via Randomized Smoothing (arXiv)
    Yuan Xin, Dingfan Chen, Michael Backes, Xiao Zhang

    Certifies and trains for cost-sensitive robustness, where misclassification harms are given by a cost matrix, by defining a cost-sensitive certified radius and adapting randomized smoothing certification, plus a training method that improves this robustness without hurting accuracy.

    Vancouver · Poster #E-701, East Exhibition Hall A-B · Thu 17 Jul, 16:30–19:00 (Vancouver time)
