  • ICLR 2026 Certified training LipNeXt: Scaling up Lipschitz-based Certified Robustness to Billion-parameter Models (arXiv)
    Kai Hu, Haoqi Hu, Matt Fredrikson

    LipNeXt is a constraint-free, convolution-free 1-Lipschitz architecture trained by manifold optimization on the orthogonal manifold with a Spatial Shift Module; it scales Lipschitz-based certification to 1-2B-parameter ImageNet models, improving certified robust accuracy by up to 8% at ε=1.

    Rio de Janeiro · Poster P4-#5206, Pavilion 4 · Thu 23 Apr, 10:30–13:00 (Rio de Janeiro time)

  • ICLR 2026 Formal explanations Provably Explaining Neural Additive Models (arXiv)
    Shahaf Bassan, Yizhak Yisrael Elboher, Tobias Ladner, Volkan Sahin, Jan Kretínský, Matthias Althoff, Guy Katz

    Generates provably cardinally-minimal sufficient explanations for Neural Additive Models using only logarithmically many verification queries in the number of features after parallel preprocessing; yields smaller explanations and much lower runtime than existing algorithms for the weaker subset-minimal variant.

    Rio de Janeiro · Poster P4-#4303, Pavilion 4 · Thu 23 Apr, 10:30–13:00 (Rio de Janeiro time)

  • ICLR 2026 Formal explanations Formal Mechanistic Interpretability: Automated Circuit Discovery with Provable Guarantees (arXiv)
    Itamar Hadad, Guy Katz, Shahaf Bassan

    Uses neural network verification to discover circuits with provable guarantees of input-domain robustness, robust patching and minimality, and links the three theoretically; experiments with verifiers on vision models yield circuits with substantially stronger robustness guarantees than standard circuit discovery.

    Rio de Janeiro · Poster P4-#4414, Pavilion 4 · Thu 23 Apr, 15:15–17:45 (Rio de Janeiro time)

  • ICLR 2026 Bound propagation & abstract interpretation Out of the Shadows: Exploring a Latent Space for Neural Network Verification (arXiv)
    Lukas Koller, Tobias Ladner, Matthias Althoff

    Refines inputs for neural network verification by transferring output specifications back through the latent space of zonotope set propagation, shrinking the input set to unsafe regions; the GPU-friendly, matrix-only tool cuts branch-and-bound subproblems and competes with top VNN-COMP tools.

    Rio de Janeiro · Poster P4-#4410, Pavilion 4 · Thu 23 Apr, 15:15–17:45 (Rio de Janeiro time)

  • ICLR 2026 Formal explanations Unifying Formal Explanations: A Complexity-Theoretic Perspective (arXiv)
    Shahaf Bassan, Xuanxiang Huang, Guy Katz

    Unifies sufficient and contrastive explanations as minimizing one probabilistic value function whose complexity depends on monotonicity, submodularity and supermodularity; global settings admit polynomial-time algorithms for neural networks, decision trees and tree ensembles, while local versions remain NP-hard.

    Rio de Janeiro · Poster P4-#4903, Pavilion 4 · Thu 23 Apr, 15:15–17:45 (Rio de Janeiro time)

  • ICLR 2026 Beyond linear output propertiesBeyond feed-forward netsProbabilistic certification Certifying the Full YOLO Pipeline: A Probabilistic Verification Approach
    Zongxin Liu, Lijia Yu, Tao Lin, Zhiming Chi, Lijun Zhang

    Probabilistically verifies YOLO object detectors against object disappearance by estimating output ranges under input perturbation distributions, formally verifying non-maximum suppression within them, and refining iteratively; it gives comparable guarantees and tighter IoU lower bounds with far fewer samples than existing methods.

    Rio de Janeiro · Poster P4-#4205, Pavilion 4 · Fri 24 Apr, 10:30–13:00 (Rio de Janeiro time)

  • ICLR 2026 Probabilistic certification Dual Randomized Smoothing: Beyond Global Noise Variance (arXiv)
    Chenhao Sun, Yuhao Mao, Martin Vechev

    Dual randomized smoothing certifies robustness with input-dependent noise variances, proven valid when locally constant, using a separately smoothed variance estimator feeding a standard smoothed classifier; on CIFAR-10 it beats prior input-dependent methods by up to 20.0% at 60% inference overhead.

    Rio de Janeiro · Poster P3-#916, Pavilion 3 · Fri 24 Apr, 10:30–13:00 (Rio de Janeiro time)

  • ICLR 2026 Probabilistic certificationFormal explanations EnsembleSHAP: Faithful and Certifiably Robust Attribution for Random Subspace Method (arXiv)
    Yanting Wang, Jinyuan Jia

    EnsembleSHAP is a feature attribution method for the random subspace method that reuses its computational byproducts, stays efficient, keeps properties such as local accuracy, and is provably robust to explanation-preserving attacks; evaluated against backdoor, adversarial and jailbreak attacks.

    Rio de Janeiro · Poster P4-#3811, Pavilion 4 · Fri 24 Apr, 10:30–13:00 (Rio de Janeiro time)

  • ICLR 2026 Formal explanations FAME: Formal Abstract Minimal Explanation for Neural Networks (arXiv)
    Ryma Boumazouza, Raya Elsaleh, Melanie Ducoffe, Shahaf Bassan, Guy Katz

    FAME computes formal abductive explanations for neural networks via abstract interpretation, progressively shrinking dedicated perturbation domains and using LiRPA bounds to discard irrelevant features; against VERIX+ it gives smaller explanations and lower runtime on medium- to large-scale networks.

    Rio de Janeiro · Poster P3-#1012, Pavilion 3 · Fri 24 Apr, 10:30–13:00 (Rio de Janeiro time)

  • ICLR 2026 Bound propagation & abstract interpretation Expressiveness of Multi-Neuron Convex Relaxations in Neural Network Certification (arXiv)
    Yuhao Mao, Yani Zhang, Martin Vechev

    Analyzes the expressiveness of multi-neuron convex relaxations for ReLU network certification, proving them inherently incomplete (a universal convex barrier), while showing completeness is reachable by adding polynomially many ReLU neurons or by partitioning the input into convex sub-polytopes.

    Rio de Janeiro · Poster P3-#1901, Pavilion 3 · Fri 24 Apr, 15:15–17:45 (Rio de Janeiro time)

  • ICLR 2026 Probabilistic certificationCertified training Certified vs. Empirical Adversarial Robustness via Hybrid Convolutions with Attention Stochasticity (arXiv)
    Joy Dhar, Song Xia, Manish Kumar Pandey, Maryam Haghighat, Azadeh Alavi, Ferdous Sohel, Wenyu Zhang, Nayyar Zaidi

    HyCAS couples 1-Lipschitz spectrally normalized convolutions with random projection filters and attention noise, yielding a randomized network that is at most 2-Lipschitz with L2 certificates; it raises certified accuracy by up to 7.3% and empirical robustness by up to 3.1% on imaging benchmarks.

    Rio de Janeiro · Poster P3-#812, Pavilion 3 · Sat 25 Apr, 10:30–13:00 (Rio de Janeiro time)

  • ICLR 2026 OtherComplete verification Parameterized Hardness of Zonotope Containment and Neural Network Verification (arXiv)
    Vincent Froese, Moritz Grillo, Christoph Hertrich, Moritz Stargalla

    Proves parameterized hardness for ReLU network verification: deciding positivity of an ℓ-layer network is W[ℓ−1]-hard in the input dimension, so zonotope non-containment is W[1]-hard; maximum and Lipschitz-constant approximation are also hard, making naive enumeration essentially optimal under the Exponential Time Hypothesis.

    Rio de Janeiro · Poster P3-#904, Pavilion 3 · Sat 25 Apr, 10:30–13:00 (Rio de Janeiro time)

  • ICRA 2026 NN control systems Formal Safety Verification and Refinement for Generative Motion Planners via Certified Local Stabilization
    Devesh Nath, Haoran Yin, Glen Chou

    Certifies closed-loop safety of generative motion planners by stabilizing sampled references with a small neural tracking controller and applying neural network verification to obtain reachable sets; verified references are reused online, improving safety for diffusion, flow-matching and vision-language planners in simulation and hardware.
