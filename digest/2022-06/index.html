IROS 2022 and OOPSLA 2022 decisions: proof transfer is a common thread, with FANC reusing robustness proofs for quantized or pruned networks and vegas verifying GNN-based job schedulers. On the control side, almost-barrier functions quantify safety of learned self-driving controllers.

3 NNV papers accepted in June 2022, at IROS 2022 and OOPSLA 2022.

OOPSLA 2022 2 papers

  • OOPSLA 2022 Bound propagation & abstract interpretationQuantized & bit-level Proof transfer for fast certification of multiple approximate neural networks
    Shubham Ugare, Gagandeep Singh, Sasa Misailovic

    FANC transfers robustness proofs from a neural network to its quantized or pruned approximate versions by building templates, symbolic shapes at intermediate layers that capture the proof; verification with DeepZ speeds up by up to 4.1x.

  • OOPSLA 2022 Beyond feed-forward nets Scalable verification of GNN-based job schedulers
    Haoze Wu, Clark Barrett, Mahmood Sharif, Nina Narodytska, Gagandeep Singh

    vegas verifies single-step and multi-step properties such as strategy-proofness, sharing incentive and stability of GNN-based job schedulers by combining abstraction, refinement, solvers and proof transfer; it gives significant speed-ups over earlier methods.

IROS 2022 1 paper

  • IROS 2022 NN control systemsBound propagation & abstract interpretation Quantifying Safety of Learning-based Self-Driving Control Using Almost-Barrier Functions
    Zhizhen Qin, Tsui-Wei Weng, Sicun Gao

    Quantifies safety of neural path-tracking controllers for self-driving by learning almost-barrier functions, certified with neural network robustness analysis in an adversarial learning-certification loop; also builds online safety monitors, tested from kinematic models up to the TORCS simulator.
